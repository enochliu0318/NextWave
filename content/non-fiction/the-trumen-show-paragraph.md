In the movie The Truman Show, director Peter Weir uses production design to show how a planned environment tries to stop a human from finding the truth. The neighborhood of Seahaven looks way too perfect with its bright colors and identical lawns. This design makes the world feel more like a cage than a real home. When a large light fixture falls from the "sky" onto the street, the cracks in the fake world start to show. This event proves that no one can hide reality forever, even with a massive budget. Weir shows that Truman feels restless because his heart knows the difference between a set and a real home. Ultimately, the filmmaker argues that humans eventually break free from any system built on lies.

Paragraph #2: Finding a Real Voice

In The Truman Show, Peter Weir uses music to show how Truman changes from a trapped man into a brave hero. During the start of the film, the background music sounds like cheesy, happy mall music. This sound choice reflects Truman’s boring and controlled life where nothing ever feels truly deep. Later, as Truman sails his boat through a scary storm to escape, the music turns into a loud and powerful orchestra. This shift in the audio helps the audience feel his growing courage and strength. By changing the style of the music, the filmmaker tracks Truman’s journey toward independence. Truman no longer follows the "script" of the show once he finds his own rhythm.
